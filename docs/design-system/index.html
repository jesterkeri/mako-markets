A note before the screens

We started Mako because the places to back a call were never built for most people.

Sportsbooks hold your money and set the odds against you. On-chain markets ask for a wallet, gas and an order book before you can even say what you think. Somewhere between those two, the simple thing got lost: you think you know how this ends, and you want to stand behind it.

So Mako keeps only what a call really is. Two sides, one pot and a clock. You sign in with an email and pick a side. If you called it, you split what the other side put in. The contract settles it in the open, and if a result never comes, everyone gets their money back without asking anyone.

This page is how we designed the screens for that. Every choice on it came from one question: would someone putting their own money on a call trust this, and enjoy it?

Direction 2a

We explored several directions for the home page and picked the one we called 2a.

People on Mako are watching a price move and a countdown run, so the screen should read like a trading terminal: mono numbers, hairline rules, dense tables, nothing decorative. But a raw terminal feels cold and hard to trust with your money, so we rounded it off: small radii on dialogs and tiles, pill controls, generous spacing and one bold display face for the moments that matter.

We do
  • Mono uppercase labels and tabular numbers so values line up and never jump.
  • Hairline rules between rows instead of boxes around them.
  • Pill controls for every choice: nav, filters, amounts, theme.
  • 12–16px radii on dialogs and tiles.
  • Big display numbers for the one figure each screen is about.
We don’t
  • Card panels stacked on card panels.
  • Colour used as decoration.
  • Full raw terminal: sharp corners, green-on-black, all caps sentences.
  • Mobile’s big rounded tiles carried onto desktop. Mobile has its own system.
  • Fake numbers. If data isn’t there yet, the screen shows a designed empty state.

What we chose, and why.

8 decisions

Two colours for the game

We kept yellow for UP and YES, red for DOWN and NO, and nothing else competes with them. An early wallet confirm used a teal success tile; we dropped it because Mako already had its colours. Sign out lost its red for the same reason: only a side gets a red fill.

Every button goes somewhere

Before a page was called done, every control on it had to lead to a designed page, sheet or state. That’s how we ended up with the claim flow, the wallet states, the 404 and every empty state.

One confirm for every transaction

Betting, creating, claiming and the faucet all go through the same confirm dialog, so people learn it once. “Free · gas covered” sits in it as a pill because not paying gas is a feature worth seeing.

Results the way the contract works

We first drew a pool flow with creator reports, challenges and bonds. The live contract doesn’t work that way, so we redrew it to match: Mako’s resolver settles, and anything unsettled after 24H refunds itself. The screen never promises something the chain can’t do.

Share the market, not the brag

For now you can only share an invite card: the bet, a QR code and a link that opens the exact market. We designed a lot of win cards and parked them all; none felt right yet.

Real data only

No fake numbers in the product. Where there’s no data yet, you see a designed empty, loading or error state with Mako, never a placeholder pretending to be real.

A tour that uses the real pages

How to play opens each page it explains: Home, Rounds, Pools, Create, Leaderboard, Me. The card sits next to what it’s talking about, and on desktop the page stays sharp behind it. We tried blurring it and took it out; the page is the lesson.

Light mode on every page

Every page was drawn in dark and light from the start, with ink outlines on filled shapes in light mode so yellow and red keep their edges on paper.

Why this frontend

Your money. Your call. A screen that tells you the truth.

The clock is running

Someone on Mako is watching a price move with money on one side. So the screen reads like a terminal: numbers line up, labels stay put, and nothing moves unless it means something.

Trust is in the details

Nothing on the screen is fake. Every button leads somewhere real. We redrew whole flows when the contract worked differently, so the page never promises what the chain can’t do.

A call should feel like play

One bold face for the moments that matter, two colours for the two sides, and a shark who shows up when something happens to you.

Mako exists so anyone can back a call and get paid fairly when they’re right. We built this frontend so that doing it feels clear, honest and fun.
